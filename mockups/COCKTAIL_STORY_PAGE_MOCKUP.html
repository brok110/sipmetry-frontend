STORY
Nothing about this drink is Alaskan. It shows up in early twentieth-century bar books — Jacques Straub's Drinks (1913) among the first — and was cemented by Harry Craddock's Savoy Cocktail Book (1930), where Craddock joked it was likelier invented in South Carolina than anywhere near the Arctic. Just gin and yellow Chartreuse, stirred cold: one of the simplest ways to let the old French liqueur do the talking.
